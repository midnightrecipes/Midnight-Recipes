Lumberjack Cake

Rated 5.0 / 5 (1 review)

Prep: 25 mins · Cook: 55–75 mins · Total: 2 hrs 20 mins+ · Serves: 8-16

A soft apple and date cake topped with a golden, caramelized coconut crust. Rich, moist, and full of warm brown sugar flavour.

Have you ever heard of a cake called Lumberjack Cake? Made with apples, dates, and a rich coconut topping, it's a wonderfully comforting cake.

Although the word lumberjack has its roots in the North American logging industry, Lumberjack Cake doesn't seem to be particularly well known in Canada. It's said to be more popular in Australia and New Zealand.

The word lumberjack also reminded me of a well-known book on personal development, The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People by Stephen R. Covey. In the book, Covey shares a story that illustrates the idea of "sharpening the saw."

A lumberjack was working hard to cut down trees with a dull axe. A passerby noticed how hard he was struggling and suggested that he take a break to sharpen his axe. With a sharper blade, he could work more efficiently and get the job done faster. But the lumberjack replied, "I don't have time to sharpen my axe! I'm too busy cutting trees." He was so caught up in his work that he couldn't see the value of taking a little time to make the work itself easier.

Covey uses this story to illustrate the importance of taking care of our greatest asset: ourselves. I think this lesson is especially relevant to our busy lives today. Technology has made so many things faster, easier, and more convenient. We can get more done than ever before, but does that necessarily mean we're any less tired? Perhaps we're simply finding more things to fill our time with.

Maybe instead of constantly trying to do more, we should make time to look after our skills, routines, systems, and surroundings. Taking care of ourselves isn't time wasted; it may be what helps us live and work better in the long run.

And perhaps this is where a comforting cake comes in!

With its soft, buttery crumb, sweet apples and dates, and golden caramelized coconut topping, Lumberjack Cake is just the kind of treat to enjoy when you finally give yourself some time to slow down!

Ingredients

THE CAKE

  • 200 g All-purpose flour
  • 300 g Apples, peeled and cored
  • 150 g Pitted dates, chopped
  • 115 g Unsalted butter, softened
  • 100 g Granulated sugar
  • 57 g Egg
  • 180 ml Boiling water
  • 5 g Baking soda
  • 3 g Salt
  • 5 ml Vanilla extract

WOOD SHAVINGS TOPPING

  • 60 g Unsalted butter
  • 70 g Brown sugar
  • 60 ml Milk (2% or whole)
  • 80 g Unsweetened shredded coconut
  • 1 pinch Salt

Instructions

  1. GET STARTED

    Preheat your oven to 350°F / 175°C. Line an 8 inch / 20 cm square baking pan with parchment paper.

    Peel and core 300 g apples, then cut them into small 5–8 mm cubes.

    Finely chop 150 g pitted dates.

  2. SOAK THE LOGS

    Place the chopped dates and the diced apples in a heatproof bowl and sprinkle with 5 g baking soda.

    Pour over 180 ml boiling water and let stand for about 10 minutes.

    Allow the mixture to cool slightly until warm.

  3. BUILD THE CAMP

    In a large bowl, cream 115 g softened butter and 100 g granulated sugar until light and fluffy.

    Add 57 g egg and 5 ml vanilla extract. Mix until combined.

    Stir in the warm date and apple mixture.

    Add 200 g flour and 3 g salt. Fold gently until no dry flour remains.

  4. SHARPEN THE SAW

    Pour the batter into the prepared cake pan and smooth the surface.

    Bake at 350°F / 175°C for approximately 35–45 minutes, until the surface is set enough to support the topping.

    The cake should not necessarily be fully baked at this stage.

  5. GATHER THE WOOD SHAVINGS

    Combine 60 g butter, 70 g brown sugar, 60 ml milk, 80 g shredded coconut and 1 g salt in a small saucepan or a microwave-safe bowl.

    Heat gently over low heat on the stovetop, or microwave in short 15–20 second intervals, stirring between each interval, until the butter melts and the sugar dissolves into a smooth syrup.

    Remove from the heat. Do not reduce the mixture too much.

  6. CROWN THE FOREST FLOOR

    Carefully remove the partially baked cake from the oven.

    Spread the coconut mixture evenly over the surface.

    Gently press the topping with the back of a spoon to help it adhere to the cake.

    Return the cake to the oven and bake for another 20–30 minutes, until the coconut topping is golden brown and caramelized and the cake is fully baked.

    Check the center with a skewer. It should come out without wet batter.

  7. REST BY THE FIRE

    Remove the cake from the oven and allow it to cool in the pan.

    Once completely cooled, carefully remove it from the pan.

    Slice with a sharp knife and serve.
