Sichuan Kung Pao Chicken

Prep: 15 mins · Cook: 10 mins · Total: 25 mins · Serves: 2-3

From a childhood murder mystery to a late-night craving: making dangerously good Kung Pao Chicken loaded with numbing huajiao, red chilies, and peanuts.

The word huajiao always takes me right back to a random detective show I watched as a child with my grandpa.

In one episode, the killer used an insane amount of numbing Sichuan pepper to numb the victim's tongue, hiding the bitter taste of poison so so the victim would finish the meal. I don't remember the actual plot anymore, but the idea that a spice could be that powerful stuck with me.

Years later, and it turns out a little bit of that numbing sensation isn't lethal at all. It's just incredibly addictive.

Tonight, I'm leaning into that craving and making Kung Pao Chicken with plenty of Sichuan peppercorns (huajiao), fresh red chilies, and crunchy roasted peanuts.　

No poison this time. Just a dangerously good late-night dinner. I hope it won't kill you..

Ingredients

THE CHICKEN

  • 454 g Chicken thighs, cut Into 2–3 cm pieces
  • 5 ml Soy sauce
  • 10 ml Shaoxing wine

AROMATICS & SPICES

  • 2 g Sichuan peppercorns (huajiao), lightly crushed
  • 5 Fresh red chilies, lightly crushed
  • 10 g Garlic, minced
  • 10 g Fresh Ginger, minced
  • 1 Scallion, cut into 1–2 cm pieces
  • 60 g Roasted peanuts, chopped
  • Neutral oil, for pan-frying

KUNG PAO SAUCE

  • 15 ml Soy Sauce
  • 15 ml Shaoxing wine
  • 15–25 ml Chinese Black Vinegar
  • 4 g Granulated Sugar
  • 5 g Cornstarch
  • 30 ml Water

Instructions

  1. MARINATE & PREP

    Place 454 g diced chicken in a bowl. Add 5 ml soy sauce and 10 ml Shaoxing wine, massaging until evenly coated.

    Set aside for 10–15 minutes.

    Meanwhile, lightly crush 2 g Sichuan peppercorns and 5 fresh red chilies. Remove the chili seeds if you prefer less heat.

    Mince 10 g garlic and 10 g ginger, and cut 1 scallion into 1–2 cm pieces.

    In a separate bowl, whisk together the 15 ml soy sauce, 15 ml Shaoxing wine, 15 ml–25 ml Chinese black vinegar, 4 g sugar, 30 ml water, and 5 g cornstarch until smooth.

    Set the sauce aside.

  2. SEAR THE CHICKEN & BLOOM THE SPICES

    Heat oil in a wok or large skillet over medium-high heat.

    Add the marinated chicken in a single layer. Sear for 3–4 minutes, turning occasionally, until golden brown on the outside. Transfer to a plate.

    Reduce the heat to medium-low. Add 2 g crushed Sichuan peppercorns, 10 g garlic, and 10 g ginger to the remaining oil.

    Stir-fry for 20–30 seconds until fragrant.

    Add the fresh red chilies and cook for another 15–20 seconds, allowing their aroma to infuse the oil without burning.

  3. TOSS & FINISH

    Return the chicken to the skillet and increase the heat to medium-high.

    Give the Kung Pao sauce a quick stir to redistribute the cornstarch, then pour it into the pan.

    Toss everything together for 1–2 minutes, until the sauce thickens and coats the chicken in a glossy layer.

    Add the roasted peanuts and scallions. Toss for another 10–20 seconds, then remove from the heat immediately.
