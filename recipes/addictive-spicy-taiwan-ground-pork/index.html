Spicy Taiwan Ground Pork

Rated 5.0 / 5 (1 review)

Prep: 10 mins · Cook: 20 mins · Total: 30 mins · Serves: 4

Recreating Nagoya’s famous Misen Taiwan Ramen mince at home. Fiery, garlic-packed ground pork over hot rice with a runny soft-boiled egg.

Taiwan Ramen (台湾ラーメン) at Misen (味仙) is absolute comfort food in my hometown, Nagoya, Japan. Despite the name, this fiery dish isn’t actually from Taiwan—the founder named it after his own Taiwanese heritage. It’s a true Nagoya original that evolved entirely in Japan, and the flavour is something you never really forget. Every time I go back home, I find myself drawn straight back to Misen, sitting at a noisy table surrounded by people happily sweating their way through a bowl.

What makes it so addictive isn't just the heat—it’s that intense, punchy wallop of fresh chili and minced garlic seamlessly blended into rich, savory pork fat. It’s crazy spicy, but there’s this deep, craveable umami underneath that keeps you reaching for the next bite before the last one even settles.

Tonight, I’m taking that iconic flavour and making the ground pork the star, serving it over hot rice with a rich soft-boiled egg. As you break the egg, the creamy, runny yolk flows into the spicy mince, perfectly cutting through the fiery punch and rounding out all those sharp garlic notes. It’s dangerous, intensely savory, and so good it might just keep me up all night.

Ingredients

TAIWAN MINCE

  • 454 g Ground pork (regular)
  • 20 g Garlic, finely minced
  • 3–5 Fresh red chilies, finely minced, seeds included
  • 45 ml Sesame oil
  • 30 ml Soy sauce
  • 30 ml Shaoxing wine
  • 15 ml Doubanjiang
  • 10 ml Oyster sauce
  • 10 ml Gochujang
  • 2 g Granulated sugar
  • 5 ml Chicken bouillon powder
  • 180 ml Water
  • 55 g Garlic chives or green onion, cut into 2 inch / 5 cm pieces
  • 3–5 ml Sesame oil, for finishing

TO SERVE

  • Steamed rice
  • Soft-boiled egg

Instructions

  1. BUILD THE CHILI-GARLIC OIL

    Add 45 ml sesame oil, minced 20 g of garlic, and 3–5 finely minced fresh red chilies to a cold frying pan.

    Turn the heat to low and gently cook for 1–2 minutes.

    Keep the garlic pale. You want the oil infused with garlic and chili, not burnt garlic.

  2. COOK THE PORK

    Increase the heat to medium-high and add 454 g ground pork.

    Let the pork sit against the pan briefly so it develops some browned edges.

    Break it apart and continue cooking until deeply browned and the pork fat has rendered.

    Push the pork toward one side of the pan. Add 15 ml doubanjiang to the exposed oil.

    Fry the doubanjiang for 30–45 seconds, then mix it thoroughly into the pork.

  3. BRAISE & REDUCE

    Add 30 ml Shaoxing wine and let it bubble for about 30 seconds.

    Add 30 ml soy sauce, 10 ml oyster sauce, 10 ml gochujang, 2 g sugar, 5 g chicken bouillon powder, and 180 ml water.

    Stir everything together and bring to a simmer. Reduce the heat to low and simmer uncovered for 10–15 minutes.

    Do not completely dry out the mince.

    The finished meat should be deeply colored, glossy and intensely savory, with a small amount of concentrated sauce and pork fat still coating the pan.

  4. FINISH WITH GARLIC CHIVES

    Add 55 g garlic chives.

    Toss over medium heat for 30–45 seconds.

    Turn off the heat. Add 45 ml sesame oil.

  5. BRING IT HOME

    Spoon hot steamed white rice into a shallow bowl.

    Pile the Taiwan mince generously over the rice. Make sure some of the concentrated sauce drips down into the rice.

    Top with a soft-boiled egg with a runny yolk.
